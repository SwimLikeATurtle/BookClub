### BEST STORY WINS : Stories are always more powerful than statistics
- The best story wins. Not the best idea or the right idea or the most rational idea. 
	- People are busy and emotional, and a good story is always more powerful and persuasive than statistics
- Good stories have extraordinary ability to inspire and evoke positive emotions, bringing insight and attention to topics that people tend to ignore when they’ve previously been presented with nothing but facts.
- Even within a good story, a powerful phrase or sentence can do most of the work 
	- There is a saying that people don’t remember books they remember sentences
- Author’s eloquence or how you present a story matters because people are bored impatient, emotional, and need complicated things distilled into easy to grasp scenes
- No one has mastered the art of storytelling better than comedians
	- "Humor is a way to show your smart without bragging" - Mark Twain
- A few things about good stores one should remember:
    #### When a topic is complex stories are like leverage
    - Stories do more than persuade others they can help you just as much part of what made Albert Einstein so talented was his imagination and ability to distill complexity into a simple scene in his head
    
    #### The most persuasive stories are about what you want to believe is true or are an extension of what you’ve experienced firsthand
    
    #### Stories get diverse people to focus attention on a single point
    - "Great books are wine, but my books are water, but everybody drinks water" - Mark Twain
    - Mark Twain found the universal emotions that influence everyone, regardless of who they were or where they were from and got them to nod their heads in the same direction
    
    #### Good stories create so much hidden opportunity among things you assume can’t be improved
    - Visa founder Dee Hock said new ways of looking at things create much greater innovation than new ways of doing them
    - When Starting a new book or a new company there is so much more opportunity if you realize that it’s not what you say or what you do, but how you say it and how you present it
    
    #### Some of the most important questions to ask yourself are: Who has the right answer, but I ignore because they are inarticulate? What do I believe is true but it’s actually good marketing?


### DOES NOT COMPUTE : The world is driven by forces that cannot be measured
- Historian Will Durant “Logic is an invention of man and may be ignored by the universe” and it often is which can drive you mad if you expect the world to work in rational ways
- Some things are immeasurable important. But they can make all the difference in the world often because their lack of qualification causes people to discount their relevance or even deny their existence.  
	- Jeff Bezos “The thing I have noticed is when the anecdotes and the data disagree. The anecdotes are usually right. There’s something wrong with the way you’re measuring it.”
- Athletic performance isn’t just what you’re physically capable of. It’s what you’re capable of within the context of what your brain is willing to endure for the risk and reward in a given moment.
- John Maynard Keynes discovered in his works that economies are not machines. They have souls and emotions and feelings. Keynes called them “animal spirits”. 
	- Archbald Hill discovered the same but for our bodies. He called them moral factors.  “Our bodies are not machine machines, and we shouldn’t expect them to perform as such. They have feelings, emotions and fears all of which regulate what we are capable of.”
- Every investment price every market evaluation is just a number from today multiplied by a story about tomorrow
- Both in the GameStop or Lehman Brothers case, the most important variable was the stories people told themselves
- Economist Per Bylund “The concept of economic value is easy, whatever someone wants has value, regardless of the reason”
- A lot of things don’t compute. The ones who thrive long-term, are those who understand the real world is a never-ending chain of absurdity, confusion, messy relationships, and imperfect people

- How to accept that things don’t compute in this world :
	1. Realize the reason we have innovation and advancement is because we are fortunate to have people in this world whose minds work differently from ours
		- "The need for certainty is the greatest disease the mind faces." - Robert Greene
	2. Accept what’s rational to one person can be crazy to another. Everything would compute if everyone had the same time, horizon goals, ambitions, and risk tolerances. 
	3. Understand the power of incentives. People can delude not only others but themselves if there is a powerful incentive. (e.g. Mortgage brokers in 2004)
	4.	The power of stories over statistics. Stories are way more persuasive than statistics.


### CALM PLANTS THE SEEDS OF CRAZY : Crazy doesn't mean broken. Crazy is normal; 'beyond the point of crazy' is normal
- Life cycle of greed and fear
	- Assume good news is permanent -> become oblivious to bad news -> ignore bad news -> deny bad news -> panic at bad news -> accept bad news -> assume bad news is permanent -> become oblivious to good news -> ignore good news -> deny good news -> accept good news -> assume good news is permanent -> the cycle repeats

- Hyman Minsky's financial instability hypothesis : Stability is destabilizing. A lack of recessions plants the seeds of the next recession which is why we cannot get rid of them.
	- When an economy is stable people get optimistic
	- When people get optimistic, they go into debt
	- When they go into debt, the economy becomes unstable
	=> Thus, Stability is destabilizing

- The higher stock evaluations become the more sensitive markets are to being caught off guard by life’s ability to surprise you in ways you never imagined
- The irony is that when markets are guaranteed not to crash they are far more likely to crash. The mirror idea of stability causes a smart and rational movement towards bidding asset prices up high enough to cause instability.
- Modern life is about as safe as it’s ever been all the improvement over the last century has come from a decline, infectious disease
- The decline in deaths from infectious diseases has made the world less equipped to handle them psychologically
- This is why the COVID-19 pandemic was so shocking and overwhelming to most people
- Calm planted the seeds of crazy:
	- Paranoia leads to success because it keeps you on your toes
	- But paranoia is stressful, so you abandon it quickly once you achieve success
	- Now you’ve abandoned, what made you successful and you begin to decline which is even more stressful
- This happens in business invest investing careers, relationship and etc. 
- Carl Jung had a theory called enantiodromia. The idea is that an excess of something gives rise to its opposite. 
	- Example: mother nature
		- There is a long history of record rains  directly leading to record fires
		- Planting the seeds of crazy makes us fundamentally underestimate the odds of things going wrong and the consequences of something going wrong
- "At your highest moment, be careful that’s when the devil comes for you” - Denzel Washington
- We insist on knowing where the top is and the only way to find it is to keep pushing until we’ve gone too far
	1. We must accept that crazy doesn’t mean broken. Crazy is normal beyond the point of crazy is also normal.
	- People are just searching for the boundaries of what other investors are willing to believe
	2. Realize the power of enough
	- Even though there may be more potential out there, it’s fine to say I’m pretty happy with this level of risk and I’m fine just watching this gameplay out


### TOO MUCH, TOO SOON, TOO FAST : A good idea on steroids quickly becomes a terrible idea
- It is very common for people to attempt to speed up a process beyond what it can handle
	- Especially if it’s a lucrative investment or a special skill
- But there are limits to scaling
	- “For every type of animal, there is the most convenient size and change in size inevitably carries with it a change of form.“ - JBS Haldane 
	- A most convenient size: proper state where things work well but break when you try to scale them to a different size or speed
- The market nearly always reward your patience when you invest around 10 years or more
- Push past the most convenient size of business and you realize that revenue might scale, but disappointed customers scale faster
	- “Growth we now know all too well is not a strategy. It is a tactic and when on discipline growth become a strategy, we lost our way.” - Howard Schultz.
- “It does not pay to try to get the business all at once. In the first place you can’t get it so a good deal of your money is thrown away.  In the second place if you did get it, the factory could not handle it.  And in the third place if you did get it, you could not hold it. A company that gets business too quickly act just about as a boy does who gets money too quickly” - Harvey Firestone(1926)
- People handle risk and responsibility and totally different ways when a group scales from 4 people to 100 million.
	- Same for corporate culture, a management style that works brilliantly at a 10 person company can destroy 1000 person company
- Good idea sped up too fast quickly becomes a terrible idea
	- Most young trees spend their early years under the shade of their mother’s canopy. Limited sunlight means they grow slow slowly.  Slow growth, leads to dense hard wood.
	- Fast growth leads to soft wood that never had time to densify. 
- Growth is good, but forced growth, accelerated growth, artificial growth tends to backfire.
	- “The greatest impediment to creativity is your impatience the almost inevitable desire to hurry up the process express something, and make a splash” - Robert Greene
- Most great things in life from love to careers to investing gain their value from two things: patients and scarcity


### WHEN THE MAGIC HAPPENS : Stress focuses your attention in ways that good times can't
- The biggest changes and the most important innovations don’t happen when everyone is happy and things are going well. They tend to occur during and after a terrible event.
- Innovation has driven by incentives which come in many forms. 
- Many big innovations in human history were heavily influenced by the military because they were trying to solve problems to win the war. 
	- When people saw cars and airplanes in their early years, their first thoughts were ‘can we mount the machine gun on it? Can we drop bombs out of it?’
	- The problem the military have dealt with is “if we don’t figure this out right now we’re all going to die and out of Hitler might take over the world”. This fueled the most incredible problem-solving and innovation in the shortest period of time the world has ever seen
- The circumstances that tend to produce the biggest innovations are those that caused people to be worried, scared, and eager to move quickly because their future depends on it
	- Stress focuses your attention in ways good times can’t. It kills procrastination and indecision, taking what you need to get done and shoving it so close to your face that you have no choice, but to pursue it right now and to the best of your ability
- 1930s was by far the most productive and technologically progressive decade in US history
	- It was the decade that transportation blossom in the US  
	- Electrification also surged in the 1930s
		- Electricity freed up hours of household labor in a way that let female workforce participation rise
	- Another productivity surge of the 1930s came from everyday people forced by necessity to find more bang for their buck
		- Supermarkets combined bakery butcher produce under one roof, making customers pick from the shelves themselves was a way to make the economics of selling food work during a time when a quarter of the nation was unemployed. 
- Big fast changes happen only when they’re forced by necessity  it rarely happens that fast without fear as a motivator  (US creating NASA two weeks after the Soviet launched Sputnik) 
- There is an obvious limit to stress induced innovation
	- There is a delicate balance between helpful stress and crippling disaster. A lot of prevents innovation has resources or sapped, and people turn their attention from getting out of a crisis to merely surviving it.
- When everything is great when the wealth is flush when the outlook is bright when the responsibility is low and threats appear gone, you get some of the worst dumbest, least productive human behavior
- “Most successful people are just a walking, anxiety disorder, harness for productivity” - Andrew Wilkinson(entrepreneur)
- “In my experience many of the most talented people I’ve met couldn’t be described as happy. In fact, there are probably more that could be described as tortured.” - Patrick O’Shaughnessy
- No one cheers for hardship, but we should recognize that it’s the most potent fuel of problem-solving serving as both the root of what we enjoy today and the seed of opportunity for what we'll enjoy tomorrow
