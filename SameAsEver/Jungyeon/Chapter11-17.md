### OVERNIGHT TRAGEDIES AND LONG-TERM MIRACLES : Good news comes from compounding, which always takes time, but bad news comes from a loss in confidence or a catastrophic error that can occur in a blink of an eye
- Good news takes time but bad news tend to occur instantly
- New tech technologies takes years or decade for people to even notice then years or decades more for people to accept and put to use
	- A lot of pessimism is fueled by the fact that it often looks like we haven’t innovated in years, but that’s usually because it takes years to notice a new innovation
- Progress always takes time often too much time to even notice it’s happened
	- But bad news? It comes instantly so fast that it overwhelms your attention and you can’t look away.
- Making a human is incomprehensively complex, but the death of a human is really simple.
	- Death are caused by blood and oxygen deficiencies. 
- The irony is that growth and progress are way more powerful than setbacks, but setbacks will always get more attention because how fast they occur. 
	- So slow progress amid a drumbeat of bad news is the normal state of affairs
- **A lot of progress and good news concerns things that didn’t happen, whereas virtually all bad news is about what did occur**
- **It is so easy to discount how much progress is achievable**


### TINY AND MAGNIFICENT : When little things compound into extraordinary things
- People assume the biggest companies nations and innovations pose the biggest threats and create the biggest opportunities. 
- Most catastrophes come from a series of tiny risk - each of which is easy to ignore - that multiply and compound into something huge. The opposite is also true.
- The first nuclear bomb was developed to end World War II within a decade we had enough bombs to end the world  
	- But there was a weird silver lining. 
	- Countries were unlikely to use them in a battle because they raised the stake so high
	- “A war that would leave not one Rome intact, but two Carthages destroyed” - John F. Kennedy
	- People then started building smaller less deadly nuclear bombs because it lowered the part of justified use. 
	- But then small bombs increase the odds of the big bombs being used
- Big risks are easy to overlook because they’re just a chain reaction of small events each of which is easy to shrug off so people always underestimate the odds of big risks
- The most astounding force in the universe is evolution
	- The real magic of evolution is that it’s been selecting traits for 3.8 billion years
- The real lesson from evolution : if you have a big number in the exponent slot, you do not need extraordinary change to deliver extraordinary results
	- “The greatest shortcoming of the human race is our inability to understand the exponential function” - Albert Bartlett
- The most important question is not “ how can I earn the highest returns?” It’s “ what are the best returns? I can sustain for the longest period of time?”


### ELATION AND DESPAIR : Progress requires optimism and pessimism to coexist
- A big thing to know about how people think is that progress requires optimism and pessimism to coexist. 
	- The best financial plan is to save like a pessimist and invest like an optimist
	- That mix is counterintuitive, but it’s so powerful when done right
- The American dream actually may have gained popularity because things were so dire. 
- Depressive Realism : a theory that depressed people have a more accurate view of the world because they’re more realistic about how risky and fragile life is.
- You can only be an optimist in the long run if you’re a pessimistic enough to survive the short run
	- e.g. Bill Gates insisted on always having enough cash in the bank to keep the company alive for 12 months with no revenue coming in. 
	- Optimism and confidence mixed with heavy pessimism
- Rational Optimists: people who acknowledge that history is a constant chain of problems and disappointments and setbacks, but who remain optimistic because they know setbacks don’t prevent eventual progress
- The trick in any field from finance to careers to relationship is being able to survive the short run problems so you can stick around long enough to enjoy the long-term growth


### CASUALTIES OF PERFECTION : There is a huge advantage to being a little imperfect
- A common urge is to squeeze out as much efficiency and perfection as you can from whatever you’re pursuing
- A species that evolves to become very good at one thing tends to become vulnerable at another
	- Perfecting one skill comes at the expense of another skill that will eventually be critical to survival. So they are all a little imperfect.
- Not maximizing your potential is actually the sweet spot in a world where perfecting one skill compromises another
- If your job is to be creative and think through tough problems, then time spent wandering around the park etc. might be your most valuable hours
- The more precise you try to be, the less time you have to focus on big-picture rules that are more important
	- If your forecast is merely good enough, you can invest your time and resources more efficiently elsewhere
Just like evolution the key is realizing that the more perfect you try to become the more vulnerable you generally are  

### IT'S SUPPOSED TO BE HARD : Everything worth pursuing comes with a little pain. The trick is not minding that it hurts.
- One of the most useful life skill is enduring the pain when necessary rather than assuming there is a hack or a shortcut around it. 
- Hacks/shortcuts rarely exists in the real world
	- It's always been this way but I suspect its getting worse as technology inflates our benchmark for how fast results should happen
- "If you are efficient, you're doing it the wrong way." - Jerry Seinfield
	- Its counterintuitive but it perfectly highlights the danger of shortcuts
- "Every job comes with pieces you don't like. And we need to say : That's part of it." - Jeff Bezos
- Everything has a price and the price is usually proportionate to the potential rewards.
	- The price one needs to pay is not cash but probably in some form of stress, uncertainty, bureaucracy, long hours, constant doubt etc.
	- This is the overhead cost of getting ahead
- Steven Pressfield lived in a halfway house before publishing, the Legend of Bagger Vance. 
	- He said The people who lived in halfway houses were the most funniest, interesting, and the smartest people he had ever met
	- “They couldn’t hold a job because they just couldn’t take the bullshit. They were the geniuses who simply couldn’t stand everyone else else’s nonsense.” 
- If you recognize that inefficiency is ubiquitous, then the question is not “how can I avoid all of it” but “what is the optimal amount to put up with it so I can still function in a messy and imperfect world?”
- A unique skill and underrated skill is identifying the optimal amount of hassle and nonsense. You should put up with to get ahead while getting along
- Where there’s pain there’s profit
- Once you accept a certain level of inefficiency, you can stop denying it’s existence and have a clear view of how the world works
	- e.g. in the bood of a bad CEO : He lives in denial over what he thinks he’s in control of and demands unrealistic precision from subordinates who compensate by hiding bad news
- Identify the price and be willing to pay it the price for so many things is putting up with optimal amount of hassle. 

### KEEP RUNNING : Most competitive advantages eventually die.
- Cope’s Rule : a clear bias towards animals evolving to become larger over time
	- But why hasn’t evolution made every species enormous?
- The tendency for evolution to create larger species is counterbalanced by the tendency of extinction to kill off large larger species
	- Evolution encourages you to get bigger than punishes you for being big
	- It’s a telling sign of what happens in so many areas of life : competitive advantages don’t stick around for long
- The only thing harder than gaining a competitive edge is not losing an advantage when you have one
	- It’s easy to overlook how many forces pull you away from a competitive advantage once you have one, specifically because you have one.
- Five things tend to eat away at competitive advantage
	1. Being right instills confidence that you can’t be wrong
	2. Strategies that led to success at one size can be impossible at another
	3. Hard work is in pursuit of a goal, I want that goal is me the relaxation that feels so justified removes paranoia. This allows competitors and a changing world to creep in unnoticed. 
	4. A skill that’s valuable in one era may not extend to the next
	5. Some success is owed to being in the right place at the right time

- The idea that advantage has a shelf life is a fundamental part of growth. 
- Competitive advantage advantages tend to be short-lived often because their success plants the seed of their own decline
- “Keep running” just to stay in place is how evolution works
	- No competitive advantage is so powerful that it can let you rest on your laurels and in fact the ones that appeared to do so tend to see their own demise. 
- You should never be surprised when something that dominates one era dies off in the next. It’s one of the most common stories in history.


### THE WONDERS OF THE FUTURE : It always feels like we're falling behind, and it's easy to discount the potential of new technology.
- Big innovations don’t come at once, but rather are built up slowly than several small innovations are combined over time
	- All innovation is hard to predict and easy to underestimate
	- The path from A-to-Z can be so complex and end up as such a strange point that it’s nearly impossible to look at today’s tools and extrapolate what they might become
- The value of every new technology is not just what I can do. It’s what someone else with a totally different skill set and point of view can eventually manipulate it into.
	- “A book is far more than what the author wrote. It is everything you can imagine and read into it as well.” - Dee Hock
