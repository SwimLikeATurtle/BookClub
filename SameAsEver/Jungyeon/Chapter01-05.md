### INTRODUCTION : The Little Laws of Life
- This is a book of short stories about what never changes in a changing world
- History is filled with surprises no one could have seen coming.
- Change captures our attention bc it's surprising and exciting. But the behavior that never change are history's most powerful lessons bc they preview what to expect in the future.
- Jeff Bezos says the important question is "What's not going to change in the next ten years?"
	- Bezos thinks its impossible to imagine a future where Amazon customers don't want low prices and fast shipping - so he invests heavily on these two
- The universal truths are obviously the most important thing to focus on bc they don't rely on chance, luck, or accident


### HANGING BY A THREAD : If you know where we've been, you realize we have no idea where we're going 
- A big lesson from history is realizing how much of the world hangs by a thread
- Some of the most consequential changes in history happened bc of a random, unforseeable, thoughtless encounter or decision that led to magic or mayhem
- Ppl like to say "To know where we're going, you have to know where we've been"
	- But more realistic is admitting that if you know where we've been, you realize we have no idea where we're going.
- I try to keep two things in mind in a world that's vulnerable to chance and accident:
	- Prediction on how ppl behave rather than specific events
	- Every event creates its own offspring which impact the world in their own special way


### RISK IS WHAT YOU DON'T SEE : We are very good at predicting the future, except for the surprises - which tend to be all that matter
- The biggest risk is always what no one sees coming bc then no one is prepared for it
- The real definition of risk is what's left over after you've prepared for the risks you can imagine. Thus, risk is what you don't see
- The common trait of big news like COVID, the Great Depression is that they were surprises.
- The biggest news, risks, the most consequential events are always what you don't see coming
	- It is why risk can never be mastered
- There's so much we don't know, not just about future but the past
	- History knows three things : 
	1) What's been photographed
	2) What someone wrote down or recorded
	3) the words spoken by ppl whom historians and journalists wanted to interview and who agreed to be interviewed
- It's impossible to plan for what you can't imagine, and the more you think you've imagined everything the more shocked you'll be when something happens that you hadn't considered. But these two things can push you in a more helpful direction:
	1) "Invest in preparedness, not in prediction" Nassim Taleb	
	- Expectations and forecasts are two different things and in a world where risk is what you don't see the former is more valuable than the latter
    - It should feel excessive it should feel like a little too much
	2) Realize that if you're only preparing for the risks you can envision you'll be unprepared for the risks you can't see every single time.
	- Preparation should feel excessive like its a little too much


### EXPECTATIONS AND REALITY : The first rule of happiness is low expectations
- Your happiness depends on your expectations more than anything else
- "If you only wished to be happy, this could be easily accomplished; but we wish to be happier than other ppl and this is always difficult for we believe others to be happier than they are." Montesquieu
- Charlie Munger said that the world isn't driven by greed, it's driven by envy
- The idea that the typical family was better off in the 50s than now is easy to debunk
	- The Facts : Avg home was 1/3 smaller than todays, Food consumed 29% of an avg household budget compared to 13% today, etc.
- There is no such thing as objective wealth - everything is relative and mostly relative to those around you
	- Everyone thinks 'What do other ppl like me have?'
- The gap between you and most of the ppl around you wasn't that large
- We tend to take every precaution to safeguard our material possessions bc we know what they cost. But at the same time we neglect things which are much more precious bc they don't come with price tags attached (relationship, freedom, etc.)
- When asked what's his secret to living a happy life, Charlie Munger said :
	- The first rule of a happy life is low expectations. If you have unrealistic expectations you're going to be miserable your whole life. You want to have reasonable expectations and take life's results, good and bad, as they happen with a certain amount of stoicism
	- Housel's friend Brent says marriage only works when both ppl want to help their spouse while expecting nothing in return
- Its often hard to distinguish high expectations from motivation. To distinguish them apart, one must recognize two things:
	1) Wealth and happiness is a two part equation: what you have and what you expect/need
	2) Understand how the expectation game is played. It's a mental game.
	- You think you want progress both for yourself and for the world but most of the time that's not what you want. You want to feel a gap between what you expected and what actually happened. And the expectation side of that equation is not only important but its often more in your control that managing your circumstances


### WILD MINDS : People who think about the world in unique ways you like also think about the world in unique ways you won't like
- You like them bc they do things other ppl would never consider or can't even comprehend
- Unique minds have to be accepted as a full package bc the things they do well and that we admire cannot be separated from the things we wouldn't want for ourselves or we look down upon
- Extremely successful ppl have abnormal characteristics and that is why they are succesful
- Ppl who are abnormally good at one thing tend to be obnormally bad at something else. It's as if the brain had capacity for only so much knowledge and emotion
- They're the risk-reward trade-offs of the same personality trait.
- Part of this idea is realizing that ppl who are capable of achieving incredible things often take risks that can backfire just as powerfully.
- "If you're not willing to do a wholesale, 24/7, 100 percent swap with who that person is, then there is no point in being jealous." Naval Ravikant


### WILD NUMBERS : People don't want accuracy. They want certainty.
- Life is about managing probabilities.
- A common trait of human behavior is the burning desire for certainty despite living in an uncertain and probabilistic world
- Probability is about nuance and gradation. But in the real world ppl pay attention to black-and-white results.
- "In any normal person's life, miracles should occur at the rate of roughly one per month" Physicist Freeman Dyson
- A one-hundred-year event doesn't mean it happens every one hundred years. It means there's about a 1% chance of it occuring in any given year.
- Individual humans have lived about 1.2 quadrillion days. Crazy thing that have a one-in-a-billion chance of happening have occurred millions of times. But the problem is far worse now than it's ever been and will almost certainly continue getting worse
- The wider the news becomes the more likely it is to be pessimistic
- *People don't want accuracy. They want certainty.*
- A big takeaway from Prof. Philip Tetlock's research is how awful so many experts are at predicting politics and the economy.
	- "We need to believe we live in a predictable, controllable world, so we turn to authoritative-sounding people who promise to satisfy that need"
- *It often takes too long for a sufficient sample size to play out. So everyone is left guessing.*
- *Distinguishing between unfortunate odds and recklessness is hard when risk has painful consequences. It's easier to see black and white even when the odds are apparent.*
