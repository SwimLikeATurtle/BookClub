### Chapter 16. Your People Strategy
- How do I get my people to do what I want?
	- You can’t. If you want it done, you’re going to have to create an environment in which ‘doing it’ is more important to your people than not doing it. Where ‘doing it’ well becomes a way of life for them
- Work is passive without you. It can’t do anything. Work is only an idea before a person does it. But the moment a person does it, the impact of the work on the world becomes a reflection of that idea—the idea behind the work—as well as the person doing it
- You become the creator of the impact on the world of the work you do
- That a business is like a martial arts practice hall, a dojo. The true combat in a martial arts practice hall is between the people within ourselves
	- I came to understand that the hotel was the least important thing in our relationship(the boss and the manager)
- They want to work for people who have created a clearly defined structure for acting in the world. A structure through which they can test themselves and be tested
- That is what the very best businesses represent to the people who create them: a game to be played in which the rules symbolize the idea you, the owner, have about the world

- **The Rules of the Game**
	1. Never figure out what you want your people to do and then try to create a game out of it
	2. Never create a game for your people you’re unwilling to play yourself
	3. Make sure there are specific ways of winning the game without ending it
	4. Change the game from time to time—the tactics, not the strategy
	- For any game can become ordinary, no matter how exhilarating it may be at the beginning. To know when change is called for, watch your people. The trick is to anticipate the end before anyone else does and to change it by executive action
	5. Never expect the game to be self-sustaining. People need to be reminded of it constantly
	6. The game has to make sense
	7. The game needs to be fun from time to time
	- In fact, a game is often no fun at all. That’s part of the thrill of playing a game well: learning how to deal with the “no fun” part so as to retain your dignity while falling on your face
	8. If you can’t think of a good game, steal one

- The Logic of the Game (The Case of the Hotel)
	- Most people today are not getting what they want. People suffer in isolation from one another
	- What most people need, then, is a place of community that has purpose, order, and meaning. A place in which being human is a prerequisite, but acting human is essential
	- A place that replaces the home most of us have lost. That’s what a business can do; it can create a 'Game Worth Playing'

- Playing the Game
	- His hotel became a world in which the sensory experiences of his customer were greeted by a profound dedication to cleanliness, beauty, and order. But this dedication didn’t rest on a purely commercial justification but a moral one
	- The Boss communicated his idea through documented systems and through his warm, moving, and positive manner. In short, the medium of communication became as important as the idea
	- The hotel’s hiring process became the first and most essential medium for communicating the Boss’s idea
- Systematizing your business need not be a dehumanizing experience, but quite the opposite
- In order to get your people to do what you want, you’ll first have to create an environment that will make it possible

- What’s wrong with hiring experienced managers?
	- Everything’s wrong with it. Because, if you don’t know how to manage, how are you going to choose them, and how are you going to manage them?
	- You don’t need professional managers. You need people who are as personally committed to those standards as you are

- The 4 Components of Hierarchy of Systems
1. How We Do It Here
2. How We Recruit, Hire, and Train People to Do It
3. How We Manage It Here.
4. How We Change It Here


### Chapter 17. Your Marketing Strategy
- Your Marketing Strategy starts, ends, lives, and dies with your customer. So, forget about everything but your customer!
	- When it comes to marketing, what you want is unimportant. And what your customer wants is probably significantly different from what you **think** he wants.

- The Irrational Decision Maker
	- Nothing escapes the customer's sensor as it absorbs the stimuli from the environment. The sensor is your customer’s Conscious Mind.
	- But its your customer’s Unconscious Mind that has to make the decisions.
- Buying decisions are made irrationally
	- When customers say “I want to think about it,” don’t you believe him. He’s not going to think about it. The decision was made long before you ever met

- The Two Pillars of a Successful Marketing Strategy
	- Demographics and psychographics are the two essential pillars supporting a successful marketing program
	- Psychographics is the science of perceived marketplace reality. It tells you why certain demographic types buy for one reason while other demographic types buy for another
- The customer's perceptions are at the heart of your customer’s decision making process.
- An age in which your customer is deluged by so many products and promises that he becomes swamped in confusion and indecision.
	- The challenge of our age is to learn our customer’s language. And then to speak that language clearly and well so that your voice can be heard above the din.

- We have entered the “unforgiving age.” An age in which countless small businesses will either accept the challenge of an information-glutted society or be destroyed by it. The challenge of our age is to learn our customer’s language.
