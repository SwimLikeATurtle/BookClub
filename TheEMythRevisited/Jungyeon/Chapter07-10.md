### Part II: The Turn-Key Revolution: A New View of Business

### Chapter 7. The Turn-Key Revolution
- Turn-Key Revolution is a way of doing business that has the power to dramatically transform any small business from a condition of chaos and disease to a condition of order, excitement, and continuous growth
	- It is the ultimately balanced model of a business that works.
- Ray Kroc convinced Mac and Jim MacDonald to let him franchise their method. Later he went on to create the largest retail prepared food distribution system in the world.
- the genius of McDonald’s isn’t franchising itself, the true genius of Ray Kroc’s McDonald’s is the Business Format Franchise.
- If you compare less than 25 percent of franchises have been terminated in five years compared to 80-percent failure rate of independently owned businesses, and you can immediately understand the power of the Turn-Key Revolution

- Before founders believed that the success of a business resides in the success of the product it sells but as there are more and more brand names being created it became difficult to establish a secure position
	- As a result, trade name franchises have been declining over the same period that franchising in general has been exploding
- Business Format Franchise is built on the belief that the true product of a business is not what it sells but how it sells it.
	- The true product of a business is the business itself.
- Ray Kroc understood that the hamburger wasn’t his product. McDonald’s was.
	- He knew he was competing with every other business opportunity so he decided to build a systems-dependent business that did works no matter who bought it
	- He began to think about McDonald’s just like Henry Ford must have thought about the Model T
		- How could the components be constructed so that the resulting business system could be replicated over and over again as reliably
	- So he build it out of perfectly predictable components that could be tested in a prototype long before ever going into mass production
- Franchise Prototype is the secret behind the stunning success of the Business Format Franchise, the launching pad for the Turn-Key Revolution
	- McDonald’s has not only created an extraordinary business, it has created a model all small business owners can emulate
- Michael Gerber says integrity is about doing what you say you will do, and, if you can’t, learning how.


### Chapter 8. The Franchise Prototype
- To create a Franchise Prototype all assumptions are put to the test to see how well they work before becoming operational in the business.
	- Prototype acts as a buffer between hypothesis and action. Putting ideas to the test in the real world rather than the world of competing ideas.
- The system integrates all the elements required to make a business work. It transforms a business into a machine, or more accurately, because it is so alive, into an organism, driven by the integrity of its parts, all working in concert toward a realized objective.
	- 80 percent of all businesses fail in the first five years, 75 percent of all Business Format Franchises succeed
- The franchisee went through a rigorous training program before ever being allowed to operate the franchise
	- Once the franchisee learns the system, he is given the key to his own business. He finally turns the keys (Thus, the name: Turn-Key Operation)
	- Because if the franchisor has designed the business well, every problem has been thought through. All that’s left for the franchisee to do is learn how to manage the system.
- The Franchise Prototype gives :
	- To The Entrepreneur, the Franchise Prototype is the medium through which his vision takes form in the real world.
	- To The Manager, the Franchise Prototype provides the order, the predictability, the system so important to his life.
	- To The Technician, the Prototype is a place in which he is free to do the things he loves to do—technical work.
- Business Format Franchise really is a proprietary way of doing business that successfully and preferentially differentiates every extraordinary business from every one of its competitors. In this light, every great business in the world is a franchise.
	- Franchise prototype refers to the whole model business, build your company as if you were going to franchise it, so it could be copied and run by ordinary people without you.
	- Proprietary operating system is the engine inside franchise prototype: the systems and procedures that make the franchise prototype work and make it repeatable.


### Chapter 9. Working On Your Business, Not In It
- One must understand that your business is not your life. Your business and your life are two totally separate things
- Your business is something apart from you, rather than a part of you, with its own rules and its own purposes. An organism, you might say, that will live or die according to how well it performs its sole function: to find and keep customers.
- The primary purpose of your business is to serve your life, you can then go to work on your business, rather than in it
- Pretend that you are going to franchise your business
- Rules of the franchise game:
	1. The model will provide consistent value to your customers, employees, suppliers, and lenders, beyond what they expect.
		- what could your Prototype do that would not only provide consistent VALUE to your customers, employees, suppliers, and lenders but would provide it beyond their wildest expectations? = the raison d’être of one's business
	2. The model will be operated by people with the lowest possible level of skill.
		- if your model depends on highly skilled people, it’s going to be impossible to replicate. Such people are at a premium in the marketplace.
		- Think how can I give my customer the results he wants systematically rather than personally?
		- Find a system that leverages your ordinary people to the point where they can produce extraordinary results over and over again.
		- The typical owner of a small business prefers highly skilled people because he believes they make his job easier (prefers Management by Abdication to Management by Delegation)
			- Unfortunately, the inevitable result of this kind of thinking is that the business also grows to depend on the moods and motivations of its people. “How do I motivate my people?” becomes the constant question and it becomes unsustainable
	3. The model will stand out as a place of impeccable order.
	- A business that looks orderly says to your customer that he can trust in the result delivered and assures your people that they can trust in their future with you.
		- “Individuals need life structure. A life lacking in comprehensive structure is an aimless wreck. The absence of structure breeds breakdown. Structure provides the relatively fixed points of reference we need.” - Alvin Toffler, The Third Wave
	4. All work in the model will be documented in Operations Manuals.
	- The Operations Manual—the repository of the documentation—is therefore best described as a company’s How-to-Do-It Guide. 
		- It designates the purpose of the work, specifies the steps needed to be taken while doing that work, and summarizes the standards associated with both the process and the result. Your Prototype would not be a model without one.
	5. The model will provide a uniformly predictable service to the customer.
	- If the business constantly changes the customer's experience providing ununiform services, then its like telling the customer it doesn't matter what you want or like.
		- It means that the business has little sensitivity to the impact of its behavior on the customer. And by doing so, it is depriving the customer of the experience of making a decision to patronize its business for the customer's own reasons
		- It is like the Burnt Child Syndrome in psychology, where a child is alternately punished and rewarded for the same kind of behavior. This form of behavior in a parent can be disastrous to the child; he never knows what to expect or how to act.
	6. The model will utilize a uniform color, dress, and facilities code.
	- Marketing studies tell us that all consumers are moved to act by the colors and shapes they find in the marketplace. Believe it or not, the colors and shapes of your model can make or break your business!

- Go to work on your business rather than in it, and ask yourself the following questions:
	- How can I get my business to work, but without me?
	- How can I get my people to work, but without my constant interference?
	- How can I systematize my business in such a way that it could be replicated 5,000 times, so the 5,000th unit would run as smoothly as the first?
	- How can I own my business, and still be free of it?
	- How can I spend my time doing the work I love to do rather than the work I have to do?

- Until you change, the problem isn’t your business; The problem is you
- To successfully develop a serious business you need a process, a practice, by which to obtain that information and, once obtained, a method with which to put that information to use in your business productively


### Part III: Building a Small Business That Works!

### Chapter 10. The Business Development Process
- "Tolerance for failure is a very specific part of the excellent company culture—and that lesson comes directly from the top." - Thomas J. Peters and Robert H. Waterman Jr., In Search of Excellence
- Business Development Process is a continuous process of building the Prototype of your business
- Its foundation is three distinct yet thoroughly integrated activities through which your business can pursue its natural evolution: Innovation, Quantification, and Orchestration

1. **Innovation**
	- “Creativity thinks up new things. Innovation does new things.” - Theodore Levitt, Harvard Professor
	- To the franchisor, the entire process by which the business does business is a marketing tool, and what it differentiates between his business and other businesses
	- Where the business is the product, how the business interacts with the consumer is more important than what it sells.
	- Innovation must always take the customer’s point of view and it should simplify your business to its critical essentials. It should make things easier for you and your people in the operation of your business; otherwise it’s not Innovation but complication.
	- Innovation produces a high level of energy in every company
	- Examples:
		1) Instead of asking “May I help you?” to a customer who visited the store, ask “Hi, have you been in here before?”
			- According to the answer you can say, “Great. We’ve created a special new program for people who have/haven’t shopped here before. Let me take just a minute to tell you about it.” => Increase in sales
		2) Wear a navy blue suit, a good, starched white shirt, a tie with red in it (a pin or a scarf with red in it for women), and highly polished black shoes. => Increase in sales
		3) If you want somebody to do something for you, touch him softly on the arm as you ask him to do it. => Increase in sales

2. **Quantification**
	- For innovation to be effective, all Innovations need to be quantified
	- Without Quantification, we dont know whether the innovation has worked or not
		- Quantification means the numbers related to the impact an Innovation makes
	- Begin by quantifying everything related to how you do business.
	- Without the numbers you can’t possibly know where you are, let alone where you’re going. With the numbers, your business will take on a totally new meaning.

3. **Orchestration**
	- Once you innovate a process and quantify its impact on your business, it’s time to orchestrate the whole thing.
		- “Discretion is the enemy of order, standardization, and quality.” - Theodore Levitt, Harvard Professor
		- If you haven’t orchestrated it, you don’t own it!
	- In short, the definition of a franchise is simply your unique way of doing business.
	- Orchestration is the glue that holds you fast to your customers’ perceptions.

- The Business Development Process is not static. When it doesn’t work any longer, change it!
	- In other words, once you’ve innovated, quantified, and orchestrated something in your business, you must continue to innovate, quantify, and orchestrate it.
- You can’t understand the value of an entire process by separating it from its parts, once you take a process apart, there is no process.
- But with the process, with the continuous Innovation and Quantification that precedes the Orchestration and that follows it. The work itself becomes something other than a habit; it becomes an exploration into who we are and how we express ourselves in relationship to something much larger
- The craftsperson learns that within the work she does there is a jewel hiding below the surface.
- For the master, there is only one way and that is to teach another.
- Efficiency, effectiveness, more money is not what the process is about. The heart of the process is to
simply and finally create more life for everyone who comes into contact with the business, but most of all, for you, the person who owns it.
