### Preface : Zero to One
- The best paths are new and untried
- Humans are the only animals that can invent new things and better ways of making them
- Zero to One is about how to build companies that create new things
- Successful people find value in unexpected places and they do this by thinking about business from first principles instead of formulas.

### Chapter 1 : The Challenge of the Future
- Brilliant thinking is rare, but courage is in even shorter supply than genius

#### Zero to One : The Future of Progress
- Globalization (copying things that work) = horizontal progress vs Technology (doing new things) = vertical progress
	- Properly understood, any new and better way of doing things is technology
	- Thiel thinks Technology matters more than globalization
		- Bc globalization w/o new tech is unsustainable
- Today our challenge is to both imagine and create the new techs that can make the 21st century more peaceful and prosperous than the 20th

#### Startup Thinking
- A startup is the largest group of ppl you can convince of a plan to build a different future
- A new company's most important strength is new thinking
- This book is about the questions you must ask and answer to succeed in the business of doing new things
	- It is not a manual but rather a exercise in thinking bc that is what startups do : question received ideas and rethink business from scratch


### Chapter 2 : Party Like It's 1999
- Companies exists to make money, not to lose it -> wasn't a conventional belief in the 90s
- 1st step to thinking clearly is to question what we think we know about the past

#### A Quick History of the 90s
- In the 90s, US was in recession, internet had yet to take off, East Asian financial crises, the launch of euro in EU => back drop of short-lived dot-com mania that begun in Sept. 1998

#### Mania : September 1998 - March 200
- Time of Silicon Valley gold rush
	- Every week there were dozens of new startups throwing launch parties
	- Ppl decamping well-paying jobs to found startups

#### PayPal Mania
- PayPal's mission : To create a new internet currency to replace the US dollar
- Began email payment service but growth was slow
- Paid new customers $10, also paid $10 for referrals -> was able to get new customers at exponential rate
	- this customer acquisition strategy is unsustainable bc here exponential growth means exponentially growing cost structure
- PayPal knew that the boom was going to end so they wanted to move fast and raise funds while they could
- Just as PayPal closed the deal in the March 2000 financing round, the bubble popped

#### Lessons Learned
- Entrepreneurs learned lessons 1-4 that became dogma in the startup world but Thiel thinks opposite principles(marked in italic) are more correct: 
	1) Make incremental advances : small incremental steps are the only way forward
	-> ***It is better to risk boldness than triviality***
	2) Stay lean and flexible : planning is arrogant and inflexible, lean means to try and iterate
	-> ***A bad plan is better than no plan***
	3) Improve on the competition : serve existing customers, build company by improving services already offered by successful competitors
	-> ***Competitive markets destroy profits***
	4) Focus on the product, not sales : if ur product needs ads or sales ppl its not good enough
	-> ***Sales matters just as much as product***

- How to be contrarian : Do not oppose the crowd but think for yourself
	- Ask yourself how much of what you know about biz is shaped by mistake? 


### Chapter 3 : All Happy Companies are Different

- Biz ver. of contrarian Q : What valuable company is nobody building?
- Biz needs to create value and also become valuable itself
	- airlines vs. google : airline creates more value but their BM is less profitable(captures less value)
-  Perfect competition vs monopoly
	- Perfect competition used to be considered ideal and default state in Econ 101. But under perfect competiton, no company makes an economic profit in the long run
- Capitalism and competition are opposites. Bc under perfect competition all profits get competed away
- If you want to create and capture lasting value, don't build an undifferentiated commodity biz

#### Lies People Tell
- There's an enormous difference between perfect competition and monopoly and most businesses are much closer to one extreme than we commonly realize

#### Monopoly Lies
- Monopolists lie and doesn't brag about their monopoly to protect themselves from audits and scrutiny
	-> It helps them escape unwanted attention

#### Competitive Lies
- Non-monopolists are always biased to understate the scale of their competition 
- If you lose sight of competitive reality and focus on trivial differentiating factors your business is unlikely to survive
	- e.g. Creating a new Indian resto with a superior naan recipe downtown where there are many restos
- Non-monopolists exaggerate their distinction by defining their market as the intersection of various small markets
	-  Monopolists disguise their monopoly by framing their markets as the union of several large markets

#### Ruthless People
- Intense competition means squeezing out every efficiency due to price competiton and low margins and a culture of ruthlesstnes
- Monopoly, on the other hand, has wider latitude to care for its workers, product, and impact in the world bc it is not competing with anyone
	- Monopolists can afford to think about things other than making money

#### Monopoly Captialism
- Creative monopolists give customer more choices by adding entirely new categories of abundance to the world. They are powerful engines for making society better
- Monopolies drive progress bc monopoly profits work as a great incentive to innovate. Also the profits help them make long term plans and finance them
- *Monopoly is the condition of every successful business*

### Chapter 4 : The Ideology of Competition
- They do people think competition is healthy?
	- Bc competition is not just an economic concept or a simple inconvenience that individuals and companies must deal with in the market

#### War and Peace
- Why do people compete with each other?
	- Shakespear best put it by saying all combatants look more or less alike. But they hate each other and become obsessed with each other which does not end well for both parties (e.g. Romeo and Juliet)
- Competition can make ppl hallucinate opportunities where none exists
	- e.g. MS vs Google, Oracle vs Siebel
- If you can recognize competition as a destructive force instead of a sign of value, you're already more sane than most
