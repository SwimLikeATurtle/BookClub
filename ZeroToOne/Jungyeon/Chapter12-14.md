### CHAPTER 12 : MAN AND MACHINE
- Computers have already enough power to outperform ppl in activities we used to think of as distinctively human 
- VC Andy Kessler said the best way to create productivity is to get rid of people
- Thiel thinks computers are complements for humans not substitutes

#### SUBSTITUTION VS. COMPLEMENTARITY
- Amercans fear technology in the near future bc they see it as a replay of the globalization in the past. But the situations are very different: ppl compete for jobs and for resources; computers compete for neither
 
##### Globalization Means Substitution
- Gains from trade are the greatest when there's a big discrepency in comparative advantage but the global supply of workers willing to do repetitive tasks for an extremely small wage is extremely large
- People don't just compete to supply labor they also demand the same resources.
- Desire doesn't stop at subsistence - people will damand ever more as globalization continues

##### Technology Means Complementarity
- Men and machines are good at fundametally different things (= Moravec's Law)
	- They excel at efficient data processing but they struggle to make basic judgements that would be simple for any human
- Gains from working with computers are much higher than gains from trade with other people. Because computers are tools not rivals
- When we design new tech, we get all the efficiency gains of a hyperspecialized trading partner w/o having to compete with it for resources
	- tech is the one way for us to escape competition in a globalizing world

#### COMPLEMENTARY BUSINESS
- Complementarity between computers and humans is the path to building a great biz
- Max Levchin assembled an elite team of mathematicians to weed out the fraudulent transfers at PayPal. 
	- They wrote software to automatically identify and cancel bogus transactions in real time. But the enemies were adaptive but the software couldn't adapt in response.
	- The engineers rewrote the software, Igor to take a hybrid approach: the computer would flag the most suspicious transactions on a well-designed user interface, and human operators would make the final judgment as to their legitimacy.
	- The human-computer hybrid approach from PayPal's security system to identify terrorist networks and financial fraud. We already knew the FBI was interested, and in 2004 we founded Palantir, a software company that helps people extract insight from divergent sources of information
- Advanced software made this possible, but even more important were the human 'professionals' without whose active engagement the software would have been useless

##### The Ideology of Computer Science
- Computer scientists try hard to reduce human capabilities into specialized tasks that computers can be trained to conquer one by one
- Companies mistakenly believe that more data always creates more value. Thiel thinks big data is usually dumb data.
	- Actionable insights can only come from a human analyst (or the kind of generalized artificial intelligence that exists only in science fiction).
- But the most valuable companies in the future won't ask what problems can be solved with computers alone
	- Instead, they ask: *how can computers help humans solve hard problems?*

#### EVER-SMARTER COMPUTERS : FRIEND OF FOE?
- The logical endpoint to substitutionist thinking is called 'strong AI'
	- Strong AI: computers that eclipse humans on every important dimension
- Technology is supposed to increase our mastery over nature and reduce the role of chance in our lives
- But even if strong Al is a real possibility, it won't happen anytime soon
	- Thiel thinks replacement by computers is a worry for the 22nd century
- As we find new ways to use computers, they won't just get better at the kinds of things people already do; they'll help us to do what was previously unimaginable


### CHAPTER 13 : SEEING GREEN
- At the start of the 21st century, everyone agreed that the next big thing was clean technology
	- Entrepreneurs started thousands of cleantech companies and investors poured more than $50 billion into them
	- Instead of a healthier planet, we got a massive cleantech bubble
- Most cleantech companies crashed because they neglected one or more of the seven que crashed Devery business must answer. Whatever your industry, any great business plan must address every one of them

#### THE ENGINEERING QUESTION
- Cleantech companies rarely produced 2x, let alone 10x, improvements. Sometimes their offerings were actually worse than the products they sought to replace
- Companies must strive for 10x better because merely incremental improvements often end up meaning no improvement at all for the end user

#### THE TIMING QUESTION
- Entering a slow-moving market can be a good strategy, but only if you have a definite and realistic plan to take it over

#### THE MONOPOLY QUESTION
- Customers won't care about any particular technology unless it solves a particular problem in a superior way
- They would rhetorically shrink their market in order to seem differentiated, only to turn around and ask to be valued based on huge, supposedly lucrative markets

#### THE PEOPLE QUESTION
- The all failed cleantech companies were run by nontechnical teams
	- The salesman execs were good at raising money but not building products that customers want

#### THE DISTRIBUTION QUESTION
- Cleantech companies effectively courted gov and investors but they often forgot about customers
	- e.g. The electric vehicle startup Better Place made it overly complicated for customers to purchase their vehicles

#### THE DURABILITY QUESTION
- Every entrepreneur should plan to be the last mover in their market
	- Ask urself what will the world look like in 10 and 20 yrs from now and how will my business fit in?
- Many of the failed cleantech companies blamed low cost Chinese competitor for their bankruptcy but Thiel thinks that it was predictable
	- They should have asked themselves : "What will stop China from wiping out my business?"

#### THE SECRET QUESTION
- Great companies have specific reasons for sucess that other ppl don't see

#### THE MYTH OF SOCIAL ENTREPRENEURSHIP
- Social entrepreneurs aim to combine the best of both worlds (profit and public interest) and do well by doing good. But they end up doing neither.
	- Thiel thinks the definition of 'social' is ambiguous
- Doing sth different is whats truly good for society

#### TESLA : 7 FOR 7
- Tesla is one of the few cleantech companies thats still thriving today
	- They got the 7 questions right
		- Distribution : Tesla took it seriously that it owns the entire distribution system
		- Secrets : Tesla knew that fashion drove interest in cleantech so it built cars that look cool

#### ENERGY 2.0
- An entrepreneur can't benefit from macro-scale insight unless his own plans begin at the micro-scale
- The challenge for the entrepreneurs who will create Energy 2.0 is to think small


### CHAPTER 14 : THE FOUNDER'S PARADOX
- This chapter is about why it is more powerful and dangerous for a company to be led by unusual individual

#### THE DIFFERENCE ENGINE
- Most ppl fall into the middle(avg) in a normal distribution of traits
- We expect opposite traits to be mutually exclusive but it happens all the time to founders:	- rich and poor : cash poor but rich on paper
- Founders' trait appear to follow n inverse normal distribution
- Where does the strange combo of traits come from?
	- Cycle : actually different -> develop extreme traits -> they exaggerate -> others exaggerate -> (goes on in circles)

#### WHERE KINGS COME FROM
- Like extreme founder figures, classical mythology have many figures who are paradigmatic insider/outsider
- The famous and infamous have always served as vessel for public sentiment : they're praised amid prosperity and blamed for misfortune

#### AMERICAN ROYALTY
- We alternately worship and despise tech founders just as we do celebrities
- Fallen stars like Elvis, MJ, Britney saw success at a young age but self destructed later in life
	- Similar examples can be found in tech founders : Howard Hughes
- Success can attract highly focused attacks : e.g. Bill Gates

#### THE RETURN OF THE KING
- Just as legal attacks on MS was ending Gate's dominance, Steve Job's return to Apple demonstrated the irreplaceable value of a company's founder
- Job's return to Apple 12 years later shows how the most important task in business, "the creation of new value" cannot be reduced to a formula and applied by professionals
	- Apple's value crucially depended on the singular vision of a particular person
- The companies that create new tech often resemble feudal monarchies rather than organizations that are supposedly more 'modern'
- Thus, we should be more tolerant of founders who seem strange or extreme
- Great founders are important bc they can bring out the best work from everybody
- The single greatest danger for a founder is to become so certain of his own myth that he loses his mind
	- But an equally insidious danger for every business is to lose all sense of myth and mistake disenchantment for wisdom

### CONCLUSION

#### STAGNATION OF SINGULARITY
- Philosopher Nick Bostrm describes four possible patterns for the future of humanity
	1) Recurrent collapse : Civilization repeatedly grows and then collapses. Humanity survives, but keeps falling back to a more primitive state and rebuilding
	2) Plateau : Humanity reaches a certain level of technological and social development and then stops progressing significantly. Civilization survives for a long time but never reaches its full potential
	3) Extinction : Humanity becomes extinct. There are no future generations of humans or human descendants
	4) Posthumanity : Humanity develops far beyond its current condition, potentially through technologies and eventually become 'posthuman,' possessing capabilities dramatically beyond those of present-day humans
- Thiel thinks our future would follow the Posthumanity pattern
	- He agrees with Ray Kurzweil's idea that the singularity is near
		- Singularity : the point when technology, advances beyond ordinary human intelligence and begins changing the world at an extremely rapid, difficult-to-predict pace. 
- Our task today is to find singular ways to create the new things that will make the future not just different but better - to go from 0 to 1
