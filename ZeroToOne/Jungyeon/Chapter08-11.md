### CHAPTER 8 : SECRETS
- A conventional truth(like elemetary math) can be important but it won't give you an edge.
- Biz ver. of contrarian Q : What valuable company is nobody building?
- This chapter helps you to find these secrets

#### WHY AREN'T PEOPLE LOOKING FOR SECRETS?
- People are depressed bc all the world's hard problems are have already been solved. What's left to do is easy or impossble.
	- The classic trichotomy of the easy, the hard, the impossible
- Why do people believe this?
	1) *Geographically* there aren't any unknown spaces left to explore.
	2) *Incrementalism* taught us that we need to proceed one step at a time. School rewards one only when one does a bit better than their peers. This trend extends up to the academics and their studies.
	3) *Risk aversion* : Ppl are scared of being wrong. The prospect of being lonely and wrong can be unbearable
	4) *Complacency* : Once becoming a social elite, one tends to think 'why search for it if you can live comfortably?'
	5) *Flatness* : As globalization advances people perceive the world as one homogenous and highly competitive marketplace. The toxic thought : 'Someone in this world would have invented it'

#### THE WORLD ACCORDING TO CONVENTION
- A world w/o secrets would mean that we have perfect justice and efficient market
	- Have we achieved that? No. Which means that there are secrets to be found

#### THE CASE FOR SECRETS
- You can't find secrets w/o looking for them. Belief in secrets is an effective truth. 
	- If you think sth hard is impossible, you'll never start trying to achieve it.
- The actual truth is that there are many more secrets left to find, but they yield only to relentless searchers
- Great companies can be built on open but unsuspected secrets about how the world works.
	- e.g. Uber - there are taxis and limos but looked beyond the convention to find opportunity hidden in plain sight

#### HOW TO FIND SECRETS
- The two types of secret : nature and people
	- What secrets is nature not telling you?
	- What secrets are people not telling you?
- Competition and Capitalism are opposites
- Are there any fields that matter but haven't been standardized and institutionalized?

#### WHAT TO DO WITH SECRETS
- Once you find a secret its rarely a good idea to tell ppl.
	- Best entrepreneurs know every great biz is built around a secret that is hidden from the outside
- A great company is a conspiracy to change the world; when you share your secret, the recipient becomes a fellow conspirator


### CHAPTER 9 : FOUNDATIONS
- A startup messed up at its foundation cannot be fixed (Thiel's Law)

#### FOUNDING MATRIMONY
- Choosing a co-founder is like getting married
	- If founders fall apart, the company becomes the victim
- Thiel observes the following in the founding team : technical abilities, complementary skill sets, how they know each other, how well they work together.
	- He thinks founders should share prehistory before they start a company.

#### OWNERSHIP, POSSESSION, AND CONTROL
- Its hard to go from 0 to 1 without a team
	- A sole proprietorship has limits
- It’s useful to distinguish between three concepts :
	1) Ownership : who legally owns a company’s equity (founders, employees, and investors)
	2) Possession : who actually runs the company on a day-to-day basis (managers, and employees)
	3) Control : who formally governs the company’s affairs (BoD including founders and investors)
	- In theory, the distinction would be carried out smoothly, but in actuality it also multiplies opportunities for misalignment
- The CEO of a huge company will own some of the company stock, but only a trivial portion of the total. Therefore he’s incentivized to reward himself through the power of possession rather than the value of ownership.
	- If stock comes as a reward for short term performance, the CEO will find a more lucrative and much easier to cut cost instead of investing in innovative long-term goals
- Most conflicts in a startup erupt between ownership and control. conflict increases over time as interest diverge
- The smaller the board, the easier it is for the directors to communicate to reach consensus and to exercise effective oversight. 

#### ON THE BUS OR OFF THE BUS
- Everyone you involve with your company should be involved full-time.
- Anyone who doesn't own stock options or draw a regular salary from your company is fundamentally misaligned.

#### CASH IS NOT KING
- A company does better the less it pays the CEO
	- High pay incentivizes the CEO to defend the status quo along with his salary, not to work with everyone else to surface problems and fix them aggressively.
- Low CEO pay also sets the standard for everyone else. 
	- If a CEO doesn't set an example by taking the lowest salary in the company, he can do the same thing by drawing the highest salary.
	- High cash compensation teaches workers to claim value from the company as it already exists instead of investing their time to create new value in the future.

#### VESTED INTERESTS
- Equity is the one form of compensation that can effectively orient people toward creating value in the future.
	- However, you must allocate it carefully and it's impossible to achieve perfect fairness when distributing
- Equity is a powerful tool precisely the best way for a founder to keep everyone in the company broadly aligned.

#### EXTENDING THE FOUNDING
- The founding moment of a company happen just once
	- Only at the very start do you have the opportunity to set the rules that will align people toward the creation of value in the future.
- Founding lasts as long as a company is creating new thing

### CHAPTER 10 : THE MECHANICS OF MAFIA
- This chapter goes into what the ideal company culture looks like
- "Company culture" doesn't exist apart from the company itselt: no company has a culture; every company is a culture.
- Silicon Valley companies are famous for their absurd perks but none of the substance. without substance perks don't work.

#### BEYOND PROFESSIONALISM
- Thiel thinks "Why work with a group of people who don't even like each other?"
- Thiel wanted PayPal to be tightly knit instead of transactional.
	- Stronger relationships = successful careers)
- He hires people who would actually enjoy working together.

#### RECRUITING CONSPIRATORS
- Recruiting is a core competency for any company.
- The founder must think "Why should the 20th employee join your company?"
- Talented people don't need to work for you; they have plenty ot options.
- Why a recruit should join your company instead of many others?
	- The two general kinds of good answers are : answers about your mission and answers about your team. but even a great mission is not enough.
- You should be able to explain why your company is a unique match for the person personally. And if you can't do that, he's probably not the right match.
	- Promise what no others can: the  opportunity to do irreplaceable work on a unique problem alongside great people.

#### WHAT'S UNDER SILICON VALLEY'S HOODIES
- Tech workers wear tshirts with the logos of their companies they work for. The branded T-shirt or hoodie that makes him look the same as his co-workers. 
	- The startup uniform encapsulates a simple but essential principle: everyone at your company should be different in the same way—a tribe of like-minded people fiercely devoted to the company's mission.
- Startups must work quickly and efficiently in order to survive, and that's easier to do when everyone shares an understanding
	- Max Levchin, co-founder at PayPal, says that startups should make their early staff as personally similar as possible. 

#### DO ONE THING
- Thiel made sure every person at PayPal was responsible for doing just one thing. Every employee's one thing was unique.
	- This simplified the task of managing people and defined roles reduced conflict.
- Most fights inside a company happen when colleagues compete for the same responsibilities.
- Internal peace is what enables a startup to survive at all.
	- Internal conflict is like an autoimmune dis-ease: the technical cause of death may be pneumonia, but the real cause remains hidden from plain view.

#### OF CULTS AND CONSULTANTS
- The extreme opposite of a cult is a consulting firm
	- Not only does it lack a distinctive mission of its own, but individual consultants are regularly dropping in and out of companies to which they have no long-term connection whatsoever.
- The difference between cults and startups is that cults tend to be fanatically wrong about something important. People at a successful startup are fanatically right about something



### CHAPTER 11 : IF YOU BUILD IT, WILL THEY COME?
- People(leaders and workers) think salespeople and other "middlemen" supposedly get in the way, and distribution should flow magically from the creation of a good product.
	- But customers will not come just because you build it. You have to make that happen, and it's harder than it looks.

#### NERDS VS. SALESMEN
- Advertising doesn't exist to make you buy a product right away; it exists to embed subtle impressions that will drive sales later.
- In engineering, a solution either works or it fails. You can evaluate someone else's work with relative ease. Sales is the opposite: an orchestrated campaign to change surface appearances without changing the underlying reality. This strikes engineers as trivial if not fundamentally dishonest.
	- What nerds miss is that it takes hard work to make sales look easy.

#### SALES IS HIDDEN
- All salesmen are actors: their priority is persuasion, not sincerity.
- Sales works best when hidden.
- Whatever the career, sales ability distinguishes superstars from also-rans.
- Businesspeople underestimate the importance of sales is the systematic effort to hide it at every level of every field in a world secretly driven by it 
- Its better to think of distribution as something essential to the design of your product.

#### HOW TO SELL A PRODUCT
- Superior sales and distribution by itself can create a monopoly, even with no product differentiation.
- In general, the higher the price of your product, the more you have to spend to make a sale
- The total net profit that you earn on average over the course of your relationship with a customer (Customer Lifetime Value, or CLV) must exceed the amount you spend on average to acquire a new customer (Customer Acquisition Cost, or CAC).

    *Complex Sales*
- If your average sale is seven figures or more, every detail of every deal requires close personal attention. It might take months to develop the right relationships. You might make a sale only once every year or two. Then you'll usually have to follow up during installation and service the product long after the deal is done.
- Complex sales works best when you don't have 'salesmen' at all.
- At high price point, buyers want to talk to the CEO, not the VP of Sales.
- Businesses with complex sales models succeed if they achieve 50% to 100% year-over-year growth over the course of a decade.
- Good enterprise sales strategy starts small, as it must: a new customer might agree to become your biggest customer, but they'll rarely be comfortable signing a deal completely out of scale
- Once you have a pool of reference customers who are successfully using your product, then you can begin the long and methodical work of hustling toward ever bigger deals.

    *Personal Sales*
- The challenge here isn't about how to make any particular sale but how to establish a process by which sale team of modest size can move the product to a wide audience.
	- e.g. In 2009, Box sold a small account to the Stanford Sleep Clinic, today, Stanford Hospital runs on Box.
	- A complex sales approach would have made Box a forgotten startup failure; instead, personal sales made it a multibillion-dollar business.
	- e.g. Zocdoc, by adding doctors to the network, salespeople make the product more valuable to consumers (and more consumer users increases its appeal to doctors).

    *Distribution Doldrums*
- In between personal sales (salespeople obviously required) and traditional advertising (no salespeople required) there is a dead zone.
- For a product priced around $1,000, there might be no good distribution channel to reach the small businesses that might buy it.
	- They simply don't have the resources to send an actual person to talk to every prospective customer. Distribution is the hidden bottleneck.

    *Marketing and Advertising*
- Advertising can work for startups, too, but only when your customer acquisition costs and customer lifetime value make every other distribution channel uneconomical.

    *Viral Marketing*
- A product is viral if its core functionality encourages users to invite their friends to become users too.
- The ideal viral loop should be as quick and frictionless as possible.
- Once PayPal dominated the segment and became the payments platform for eBay, there was no catching up

    *The Power Law of Distribution*
- The kitchen sink approach doesn't work. 
	- e.g. Employ a few salespeople, place some magazine ads, and try to add some kind of viral functionality to the product as an afterthought
- Poor sales rather than bad product is the most common cause of failure.

    *Selling to Non-Customers*
- It is a LIE that great products sell themselves: 
	- This company is so good that people will be clamoring to join it.
	- This company is so great that investors will be banging down our door to invest.
- Sales rarely happen without calculated recruiting and pitching beneath the surface.
- You should never assume that people will admire your company without a public relations strategy.
	- The press can help attract investors and employees.

#### EVERYBODY SELLS
- If you don't see any sales-people, you're the salesperson.
