### CHAPTER 5 : LAST MOVER ADVANTAGE
- A great biz is defined by its ability to generate cash flows in the future.
	- The value of a biz today is the sum of all the money it will make in the future
		- To properly value a biz, you must discount future cash flows to the present worth. Comparing discounted cash flows shows the difference between low-growth biz and high-growth start ups.
- Tech companies lose money for the first few years.
	- It takes time to build valuable things and that means delayed revenue.
- For a company to be valuable it must grow and endure, but many entrepreneurs focus only on the short-term growth
	- If you focus on near-term growth above all else, you miss the most important question: ***will this biz still be around a decade from now?***
	- Numbers alone cannot answer this question. You must critically think about the qualitative characteristics of your biz
 

#### CHARACTERISTICS OF MONOPOLY
- Analyzing ur biz according to the following charactertistics can help you think about how to make it durable

#### **1. Proprietary Technology**
- Its the most substansive advantage a company can have bc it makes ur product difficult or impossible to replicate
	- Proprietary tech must be at least 10x better than its closest substitute in some important dimension to lead to a real monopolistic advantage
- The clearest way to make a 10x improvement is to invent sth completely new.
	- e.g. PayPal made buying and selling on eBay 10x better
		Amazon provided 10x as many books as any other store
		iPad made tablets 10x better thru superior integrated design

#### **2. Network Effects**
- Network effects make a product more useful as more ppl use it.
- Paradoxically network biz must start with especially small markets
	- Initial markets should be so small that they often don't appear to be biz opportunities at all

#### **3. Economies of Scale**
- A monopoly biz gets stronger as it gets bigger
	- Fixed cost of creating a product is spread out over greater quantities of sales
	- SW biz enjoy especially dramatic economies of scale bc the marginal cost of producing another copy of product is close to zero
- A good startup should have the potential for great scale built into its first design


#### **4. Branding**
- Creating a strong brand is a powerful way to claim a monopoly. However, no tech company can be built on branding alone.
	- Apple has a complex suite of proprietary techs both in HW and SW.
		- It enjoys strong network effects from its content ecosystem

#### BUILDING A MONOPOLY

#### **Start Small and Monopolize**
- Every startup should start with a very small market
	- e.g. Mistake at PayPal : first product let ppl beam money thru PalmPilots but there was no need for it. So then they moved on to serve eBay users.
	- It is much easier to reach a few thousand ppl who really needed our product than to try to compete for the attention of millions of scattered individuals.
	- The perfect target market for a startup is a small group of particular ppl concentrated together and served by a few or no competitors
- Getting 1% of a large market is not good bc cutthroat competition means your profit will be zero

#### **Scaling Up**
- Once you dominate a niche market, then you should gradually expand into related and slightly broader markets.
	- e.g. Amazon Books -> CDs, Videos, SW -> becomes general store
- There's hidden obstacles to scaling
	- e.g. eBay's auction model works best for individually distinct products and less for commodities
- The most successful companies make the core progression - to first dominate a specific niche and then scale to adjacent markets - a part of their founding narrative.

#### **Don’t Disrupt**
- Disruption distorts an entrepreneur's self understanding in an inherently competitive way.
	- If you want to make sth new, the act of creation is far more important than the old industries that might not like what you create.
	- Good e.g. Instead of aggressively fighting incumbent credit card networks like Visa in a bloody zero-sum war, PayPal initially empowered users who were underserved by them, ultimately expanding the digital payments market so much that it created a net-positive ecosystem where everyone grew.
	- Bad e.g. Napster
- Disruption attracts attention : disruptors are ppl who look for trouble and find it.


#### THE LAST WILL BE FIRST
- The first mover advantage is a tactic not a goal. What matters is generating cash flow flows in the future.
	- Being the first mover doesn’t do you any good if somebody else comes along and unseats you
- It’s better to be the last mover. Make the last great development in a specific market and enjoy monopoly profits.



### CHAPTER 6 : YOU ARE NOT A LOTTERY TICKET
- Whether success comes from luck or skill is not important. The important questions are about the future : *is it a matter of chance or design?*
	- If you believe ur life is mainly a matter of chance, why read this book?

#### CAN YOU CONTROL YOUR FUTURE?
- You can expect the future to take a definite form or you can treat it as uncertain.
	- If you see future as definite, it makes sense to understand it in advance and work to shape it
	- If you see future as indefinite ruled by randomness, you'll give up trying to master it.

#### **Indefinite Pessimism**
- An indefinite pessimism looks out onto a bleak future, but has no idea what to do about it. (e.g. Present EU)

#### **Definite Pessimism**
- A definite Pessimist believes the future can be known, but since it will be bleak, he must prepare for it. (e.g. Present China)

#### **Definite Optimisim**
- A definite optimism thinks future will be better than the present if he plans and works to make it better (e.g. 17th century US)
	- People welcomed big bold plans and asked if it would work

#### **Indefinite Optimisim**
- An indefinite optimism thinks future will be better but he doesn't know how exactly so he won't make any specific plans
- Baby boomers learned from childhood to overrate the power of chance and underrate the importance of planning


#### OUR INDEFINITELY OPTIMISTIC WORLD

#### **Indefinite Finance**
- In an indefinite world, people actually prefer unlimited optionality; money is more valuable than anything you could possibly do with it.

#### **Indefinite Politics**
- We are more fascinated today by statistical predictions of what the country will be thinking in a few weeks' time than by visionary predictions of what the country will look like 10 or 20 years from now.

#### **Indefinite Philosophy**
- In philosophy, politics, and business arguing over process has become a way to endlessly defer making concrete plans for a better future.

#### **Indefinite Life**
- The best minds of the Renaissance thought of death as sth to defeat.
	- On the other hand, our society today is permeated by the ideas that death is both inevitable and random
- Eroom's law observes that then number of drugs approved per B $s spent on R&D has halved every nine years.

#### IS INDEFINITE OPTIMISM EVEN POSSIBLE?
- Indefinite optimism is the only one that seems to be inherently unsustainable: how can the future get better if no one plans for it?
- Darwin wrote that life tends to progress w/o anybody intending it. Every living thing is a random iteration and the best iteration wins.
- Pursing leanness by making small changes to things that already exist might lead you to a local maximum but not global maximum. It won't take you from 0 to 1.
- Darwinism may work in other contexts but in startups **intelligent design works best**

#### THE RETURN OF DESIGN
- Design doesn't mean just good aesthetics. It's long term planning
	- e.g. Steve Jobs planned the iPod to be the first of a new generation of portable post-PC device
		- While the public and media viewed the original 2001 iPod merely as a niche music accessory for Mac owners, Apple was actually laying the foundational hardware, software, miniaturization, and supply-chain building blocks for the mobile, post-PC revolution that followed.
- Founders only sell their biz when they have no more concrete visions for the company, in which case the acquirer probably overpaid.
	- A business with a good definite plan will always be underrated in a world where people see the future as random

#### YOU ARE NOT A LOTTERY TICKET
- We have to find our way back to the definite future thru cultural revolution and rejecting the unjust tyranny of chance
- Startup is the largest endeavor over which u can have definite mastery. You can have agency in ur own life and also over a small and important part of the world.

### CHAPTER 7 : FOLLOW THE MONEY
- The Pareto Principle, aka 80-20 rule surrounds us everywhere in the natural and social world.
- This chapter shows how the power law becomes visible when you follow the money

#### THE POWER LAW OF VENTURE CAPITAL
- Venture firms usually have a 10-yr lifespan since it takes time for successful companies to grow and exit
	- The error of VCs lies in expecting that venture returns will be normally distributed
	- If one focuses on diversification instead of single minded pursuit of very few companies that can become overwhelmingly valuable, one can miss those rare companies in the first place
- The biggest secret in VC is that the best investment in a successful fund equals or outperforms the entire rest of the fund combined
	- Rule #1: Only invest in companies that have the potential to reture the value of the entire fund
	- Rule #2: There are no more rules. (Since rule #1 is so restrictive.)
- VCs must find the handful of companies that will successfully go from 0 to 1 and then abck then with every source

#### WHY PEOPLE DON'T SEE THE POWER LAW
- No matter how unambiguous the end result of the power law, it doesn't reflect daily experience.
	- Since it is difficult to give up on investment, VCs usually spend more time on the most problematic companies than they do on the most successful

#### WHAT TO DO WITH THE POWER LAW
- Even the best venture investors have a portfolio, but investors who understand the power law makes as few investment as possible
- On an individual level, one should focus relentlessly on sth you are good at doing, but think hard if it is going to be valuable in the future.
-  Ppl who understand the power law will hesitate more than others when it comes to founding a new venture: they know joining a high-growth company will make them successful
- If you do start your own company, remember that :
	- One market will be better than all others
	- One distribution strategy usually dominates all others
	- Time and decision making follow a power law and some moments matter far more than others
- Because mainstream culture looks at the world through a flattened, average lens, the truly vital truths are hidden.
	- If the most important things were obvious, everyone would see them, and competition would instantly flatten them out.
- Therefore, the secrets to massive success—finding the one unique idea—look invisible or even crazy to a world obsessed with averages and safety.
